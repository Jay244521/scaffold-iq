## 1. Microfiber Care Kit — first product
**What it is:** a color-coded interior-and-exterior care kit for everyday drivers, not detailing enthusiasts.

| Item | Qty |
|---|---|
| 16×16 in, 300–350 gsm all-purpose towels (color-coded) | 6 |
| Large twisted-loop drying towel | 1 |
| Waffle or glass towels | 2 |
| Wash bag | 1 |
| "15-Minute Interior Reset" guide card with QR video | 1 |

**Variants later:**
- *Pet Owner Kit:* adds the pet-hair tool.
- *Interior Kit:* adds the brush set.

| Factor | Finding |
|---|---|
| Demand | Strong, evergreen category. The Rag Company and Chemical Guys sell bundles at $40–$97 and $45–$72 [C3]. Consumable, so customers buy again |
| Customer problem | Too many choices and no simple system (**A**, to be tested in interviews). Cheap towels that scratch or lint (common review theme; verify in reviews) |
| Supplier availability | **Best of any product.** A U.S. wholesaler lists 16×16 300 gsm towels at $0.39–$0.49 with no domestic minimum, and offers private-label logo labels (~$0.90/towel by the case) [Supplier S01, L]. Ten-plus overseas factories advertise private label at ~500 MOQ [S04–S11] |
| Landed cost | ~$9.00 (US-sourced, A/L). Falls to ~$6.88 if made overseas at scale (A), with 12.8% China duty [T5] or none from Pakistan |
| Price / margin | $29.99. Gross margin 70%. Contribution 26% on own site, 19% on Amazon, 63% local |
| Shipping & storage | Light, flat, unbreakable, no fitment |
| Returns, warranty, liability | Very low. No vehicle fit; no meaningful injury pathway |
| Regulatory | Textile labeling (fiber content, country of origin, manufacturer/RN identity) applies to some textile products. **Check with counsel whether the FTC Textile Act labeling rules cover cleaning cloths** |
| Differentiation | Weak on product, stronger on system: color-coding, guide card, QR video, a guarantee. **This is the main risk: the kit can be copied** |
| Proprietary path | Low. It is a funnel and repeat-purchase engine for the brand, not a moat |

**Why it beats the alternatives as a *first* product:** it is the only shortlisted product we can actually stock, photograph, sell and deliver inside $300 without placing an MOQ order. It also proves the brand, the channels and fulfillment before real capital is at risk.

## 2. Pet-hair removal tool — add-on
- **Problem:** pet hair woven into carpet and cloth seats. This is the most visible pain point for pet-owning SUV drivers.
- **Economics:** costs under $2.50 landed (A) and sells for $12.99. Shipping makes it weak alone; **it should ride along in a kit or with a liner.**
- **Risk:** some rubber and pumice tools carry design patents. **Run a freedom-to-operate search** on Google Patents (free) before choosing a design. Pick an unbranded generic design and let a patent attorney sign off before volume orders.
- **Why it's in the top five:** it raises order value on product #1 and introduces pet owners to the brand, the segment that buys products #3–#5.

## 3. Pet cargo liner with integrated bumper flap — backup product
- **Demand:** 4Knines dominates recommendations [C2]. Many Amazon and Chewy listings (Arf Pets, Jumbl, Smartto).
- **Complaints to fix:** low side walls, Velcro that melts in heat, suction cups that release, and sizes that are too short for full-size SUVs [C2]. Each one is a spec line in our RFQ.
- **Suppliers:** Lisohome, Pamipet, Uwin, Sanan Pet, Trimnexa and Petstar claim OEM/private label [S16–S21]. Listings show $7–$9.50 [L]. MOQ around 300 is typical in the category (**A**, confirm by RFQ).
- **Economics:** $59.99 retail, $15.70 landed (30% duty placeholder). **$18.45 contribution on own site, $11.99 on Amazon.** These are the best per-order economics on the shortlist.
- **Risks:**
  - Sizing returns (8% allowance modelled). Mitigate with a fit chart by vehicle and two sizes only (Mid SUV, Full SUV).
  - Bulky FBA fees.
  - PVC backings can trigger Prop 65 [L6]. Specify phthalate-free, non-PVC backing.
- **Why it's the backup and not the first product:** a 300-unit MOQ needs roughly $2,700–$4,700 we don't have. It becomes the first product funded by profits from product #1, or the first product if interviews show pet owners strongly prefer it and a supplier accepts a 50–100 unit trial order.

## 4. Back-seat pet hammock
- High demand and the same suppliers as #3, so the same RFQ can cover both. Sharing suppliers lowers sourcing cost.
- **Safety to specify:** seat-belt pass-throughs, side-airbag clearance (do not cover seat-side airbags), and child-seat anchor access.

## 5. Complete SUV cargo protection system — flagship
- **Concept:** a fitted cargo liner, bumper flap, seat-back protector and organizer that attach to each other and are sold by vehicle.
- **The gap:** no mainstream brand sells this as one fitted, integrated system for crossovers. That is a judgement from the competitor review, not verified market data.
- **Path to build it:**
  1. Learn sizing from liner returns and customer vehicle data.
  2. Pattern a fitted version for the top three DFW crossovers (e.g. RAV4, CR-V, Model Y; validate the list with local registration or interview data).
  3. Prototype with a U.S. short-run sewer (Big Duck Canvas, Core Products, CargoCatch [S22–S25]).
  4. Scale with an overseas sewer.
- **Why it matters:** it is the first product where we would **own the specifications and fit data**. That moves the company from reseller toward brand owner, the RealTruck/WeatherTech path.
